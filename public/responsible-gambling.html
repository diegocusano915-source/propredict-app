Understanding Problem Gambling

Problem gambling (also known as gambling addiction or compulsive gambling) is a behavioral disorder characterized by an inability to control or stop gambling despite negative consequences. It can affect anyone — regardless of age, gender, income, or background.

Gambling becomes problematic when it:

  • Interferes with work, school, or family responsibilities
  • Causes financial strain, debt, or borrowing money to gamble
  • Leads to lying about or hiding gambling activities
  • Results in chasing losses (gambling more to recover lost money)
  • Creates feelings of guilt, anxiety, or depression related to gambling
  • Damages relationships with family and friends

Warning Signs of Problem Gambling:

  • Spending more time or money on gambling than intended
  • Difficulty stopping or reducing gambling activities
  • Gambling to escape problems or negative emotions
  • Lying to conceal the extent of gambling
  • Jeopardizing relationships, job, or education due to gambling
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund gambling
  • Needing to gamble with increasing amounts to feel excitement
  • Restlessness or irritability when attempting to cut back

Practical Tips for Responsible Gambling

If you choose to engage in sports betting, we strongly encourage you to adopt these responsible gambling habits:

Set a Budget

Decide in advance how much money you can afford to lose. Never exceed this limit, and never use money meant for essential expenses like rent, bills, or food.

Set Time Limits

Decide how long you will gamble before you start. Set an alarm if needed. Take regular breaks and balance gambling with other leisure activities.

Never Chase Losses

Accept that losing is part of gambling. Trying to recover losses by betting more is dangerous and often leads to even greater losses.

Avoid Emotional Gambling

Do not gamble when you are feeling stressed, angry, lonely, depressed, or under the influence of alcohol or drugs. Your judgment is impaired in these states.

Keep It Recreational

View gambling strictly as a paid form of entertainment — like going to a concert or a sporting event. It is not a way to make money or solve financial problems.

Talk About It

Discuss your gambling habits openly with trusted friends or family. If you're concerned about your gambling, sharing your concerns can be the first step toward getting help.

Self-Assessment Questions

Ask yourself honestly:

  • Have I ever lied about how much I gamble?
  • Do I feel the need to bet larger amounts to get the same excitement?
  • Have I tried to cut back or stop gambling but couldn't?
  • Do I gamble to escape from problems or relieve negative emotions?
  • Has gambling caused financial problems for me or my household?
  • Have I borrowed money or sold possessions to gamble?
  • Do I become restless or irritable when I try to reduce my gambling?

If you answered "yes" to any of these questions, you may be at risk for developing a gambling problem. We encourage you to reach out to a professional support service.

Helplines and Support Resources

If you or someone you know is struggling with problem gambling, confidential help is available. Reach out to these organizations for support:

  • Nigeria: Lagos State Lottery Board Helpline — 0800 2255 7483
  • United Kingdom: GamCare — 0808 8020 133 (24/7 Helpline)
  • United States: National Problem Gambling Helpline — 1-800-GAMBLER (1-800-426-2537)
  • Canada: Problem Gambling Helpline — 1-866-531-2600
  • Australia: Gambling Help Online — 1800 858 858
  • International: Gamblers Anonymous — www.gamblersanonymous.org

Many jurisdictions also offer self-exclusion programs that allow you to voluntarily ban yourself from gambling establishments and online betting platforms. Contact your local gambling regulatory authority for more information.

Tools to Help You Stay in Control

Many online bookmakers and sportsbooks offer built-in tools to help you manage your gambling. We encourage you to explore and use these features:

  • Deposit Limits: Set daily, weekly, or monthly limits on how much you can deposit.
  • Time Limits / Session Reminders: Receive alerts when you've been logged in for a certain period.
  • Reality Checks: Pop-up notifications showing how long you've been playing and your net win/loss.
  • Self-Exclusion: Temporarily or permanently block access to your betting account.
  • Account Closure: Permanently close your betting account if you decide to stop.
